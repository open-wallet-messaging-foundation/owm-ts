# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be kind. Argue about the work, not the person. State your ceilings.

People who make the room worse get removed from it.

## What we expect

- **Argue hard about ideas.** A standard improves by being attacked; a
  well-aimed "this is wrong, and here is the case that breaks it" is a
  gift, and receiving one gracefully is part of the job.
- **Attack the design, never the designer.** "This construction leaks the
  sender's identity" is criticism. "You clearly don't understand
  cryptography" is not — and it is also less useful, because it contains
  no information.
- **Say what you don't know.** Nobody here is expected to have read every
  RFC. "I don't understand why this is safe" is a legitimate and valuable
  contribution, and anyone who sneers at it is the one in the wrong.
- **Assume competence and good faith**, especially across languages and
  time zones. Much of what reads as rudeness in a text box is someone
  writing in their third language at midnight.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment, discriminatory language, personal attacks, sustained
disruption, publishing others' private information, or sexualised
attention of any kind. Advocating for or encouraging any of that is the
same as doing it.

This applies in issues, pull requests, discussions, chat, and at any
event where you are representing the project.

## Reporting

Email **conduct@owm.foundation**. Reports go to the foundation's
maintainers; if your report concerns a maintainer, say so and it will be
handled by someone else.

We will acknowledge within 3 working days, take it seriously, and keep
the reporter's identity confidential. We will tell you what we decided.

## Enforcement

Depending on severity: a private word, a public correction, a temporary
ban, or a permanent one. We would rather resolve something quietly than
make an example of anyone — but a project that tolerates a hostile
contributor loses ten good ones silently, and that trade is never worth
it.
